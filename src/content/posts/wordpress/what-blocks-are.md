Every paragraph, image and heading in WordPress is its own building block. Once this idea clicks, the editor stops feeling strange.

A block is one piece of content. You stack blocks to make a page, then move, copy or change each one without affecting the rest.

Older WordPress gave you one big text box, a bit like a word processor. Adding something like two columns or a button often meant using shortcodes, special plugins or custom code. The block editor, sometimes called Gutenberg, makes these layouts available directly and shows you something close to the finished page as you work.

To add a block, click the plus button, or type a forward slash on a new line and start typing its name, such as /image. Select any block and a small toolbar appears above it. More options sit in the panel on the right.

Each block can be dragged to a new spot or moved using the arrows on its toolbar. Made a mistake? Undo works as you'd expect.

Go deeper: [Work with blocks](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/work-with-blocks/)
