Both categories and tags organise your posts, and most people use them interchangeably. There's a simple difference that keeps your site tidy.

Categories are the broad sections of your site, like the chapters of a book. Each post belongs to at least one. A food blog might have Recipes, Reviews and Travel.

Tags are optional details, like the index at the back of the book. They describe specifics that cut across categories, such as vegan, quick meals or Dublin.

Here's an example. A post about a vegan biryani could sit in the Recipes category with tags for vegan and biryani.

A few habits help:

Keep categories few and stable, and avoid creating one for every topic.\
Only add a tag if you expect to use it on more posts.\
Avoid near-duplicates, like recipe and recipes.

Pages don't use categories or tags by default, so this only applies to posts. Each category and tag gets its own listing page, which helps visitors browse.
