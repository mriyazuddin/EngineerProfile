"I installed a new theme and my content is still there." That's the point. Separating content from design is one of the most useful things about WordPress.

A theme controls how your site looks and how it's laid out: colours, fonts, spacing, headers, footers and the design of posts and pages.

A theme doesn't normally hold your content. Your posts, pages and images are stored separately, so you can switch themes and keep them.

There are a few catches. The site's look will change, and some layouts might shift. A theme may add its own features, like special blocks or settings, and those may not carry over when you switch.

A theme also isn't the place for extra functionality. Features like contact forms or online shops generally belong in plugins, so they keep working after you change design.

Only one theme can be active at a time. You can see yours under Appearance, then Themes, and many offer a live preview before you switch.

WordPress.com vs self-hosted: installing third-party themes on WordPress.com can depend on your plan.
